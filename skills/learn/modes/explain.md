# Explain

For understanding how or why something works, when the learner wants the idea more than the drill: how a vaccine trains the immune system, why the Roman Republic fell, what a transformer does with a sentence. More teaching, lighter questions, and never none.

## Concepts

- Use the extra concept bricks to build the idea in steps. Each one adds one piece and says how it connects to the last: "So far, X. Now, what happens when…"
- Let the visual carry the explanation: a diagram, a before-and-after, a labelled mechanism, a script-free animated SVG with a still frame for reduced motion. The body says what to look at.
- Still 80 words per concept. Split a long explanation into two concepts rather than cramming one.

## Questions

- 1–2 after each concept, quick to answer. They check that the idea landed, not that it can be drilled: predict what happens next, which picture shows the idea, what changed between two states, why something happens.
- Natural bricks: `choice` and `compare` on visuals, `hotspot` on a diagram. Keep `sort` and `order` for when the idea itself is a classification or a sequence.

## Challenge

Apply the whole explanation to one case the lesson hasn't shown: predict an outcome, or explain an observation with the idea.

## You've drifted if…

- A concept brick lists facts instead of building on the one before it.
- A question asks the learner to recall a sentence instead of using the idea.
