# Practice

For learning to do something: a calculation, a piece of code, a grammar form, applying a design rule to a screen. Short teaching, then many questions that take the support away step by step.

## Concepts

- 1–2 short concept bricks. Each states the method in a sentence or two, then works one example step by step: the worked example is the teaching.
- Leave out background and history; the questions are where the learning happens.

## Questions

- 3–6 after each concept, ordered so the support fades:
  1. **Finish a worked example:** a `blank` for the missing step.
  2. **Do a whole one** on new values or a new screen: a `choice` with options built from real slips.
  3. **Vary it:** a new context, a twist, or the problem run backwards.
  4. **Find the mistake** in someone else's attempt: a `hotspot` on the wrong step or element.
- Every question is a new case. Two questions that differ only in their numbers practise nothing new.
- Use realistic artifacts: a screen, a code snippet, a sentence, a table of data.
- For `intermediate` and `advanced` learners, the hook can be the problem itself, tried before any teaching: attempting a problem first helps learners who already have some of the basics. Don't do this for beginners or children.

## Challenge

A case that needs the whole method, with one twist the questions didn't have.

## You've drifted if…

- A concept brick explains why the method works at length instead of showing it.
- The last question is no harder than the first.
